Theory 001

We won't notice when AI becomes smarter than us.

It won't arrive as a moment. It will arrive as a pull.

01The thought

A fish lives its whole life inside the tide. It feels the water rise and fall twice a day. It moves with it, feeds by it, shelters from it. It never sees the moon that is pulling it.

Superintelligence will reach us the same way: as a force we feel, not a moment we witness.

We picture a day when a headline tells us the machines have passed us. I don't think that day comes. I think we just notice, slowly, that the water is moving differently.

02The argument

Intelligence doesn't announce itself. You rarely know someone is smarter than you in the moment. You find out later, when you see where they were leading the conversation.

We already hand our attention and our choices to systems we don't understand. A feed decides what we read. A map decides which street we take. A ranking decides which ideas we meet first. None of these is superintelligent, and we still can't say exactly why they show us what they do.

So the risk isn't that we fail to spot the moment AI overtakes us. By the time it does, the judgement we would use to spot it may already have been shaped by it.

03The counter

Maybe I'm wrong. We noticed nuclear weapons. We noticed the internet. Some changes are loud, and the world stops to look at them.

It's possible this will be one of those. A clear demonstration, a shock, a before and after. If so, good. A loud arrival is one we can argue about.

04What it means

The question isn't when. It's how would we know.

If we can't describe what being outthought would look like, we won't recognise it when it happens. That seems like the more urgent thing to work out.
